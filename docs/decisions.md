# Decisions — Poppy

Design judgment calls made where the brief was silent. Build-process/architecture questions are explicitly out of scope and are not logged here.

## Hero rework — product-first (current)

The client asked for a much closer match to the reference animation after seeing the first pass, with specific fixes: no visible frame around the product, the product and wordmark both much bigger, the wordmark fully opaque (not translucent), the copy block moved out of the way into a compact top-left corner with the headline rewritten shorter, a nav restyled black-transparent with white-on-hover, and the per-slide eyebrow label ("WAKE UP — BRIGHT IDEA") replaced with a plain one-sentence product blurb.

- **Fixed a real bug, not just a style request.** "Get rid of the white rectangle" was `ImageBox` rendering `bg-[var(--surface)]` behind the photo even in `bare` (hero) mode — invisible when the photo fills its box edge-to-edge, visible as a cream rectangle whenever the photo's aspect ratio left any letterboxed space (which it did, since the supplied photos are portrait bottles in a roughly-square hero slot). Fixed by making `bare` mode fully transparent.
- **Headline shortened and given its own width constraint.** The first pass's `<h1>` had no `max-w`, so at the corner position it ran the full line width instead of wrapping into a compact block — found and fixed in the same pass as the resize.
- **Product and wordmark are now bottom-anchored as a unit**, not vertically centered across the full viewport — centering a much bigger product meant its top edge collided with the nav and the new top-left copy block. Anchoring both to the bottom two-thirds of the hero keeps the top third clear for nav + copy regardless of how large the product gets.
- **Per-slide blurb field added to `HeroSlide`** — short, plain "Our X is a Y" marketing copy distinct from the product's own page voice, written to the client's own example line for Clock Out verbatim.
- **Nav pill and icon buttons restyled black/30 with backdrop blur, white text that goes solid white (pill background) on hover/active** — applied everywhere (not just the hero overlay), since the client described it as how "the nav bar" should look, not a hero-only treatment. The standalone logo keeps its existing white-on-hero / ink-elsewhere logic, since it sits outside the pill and needs to stay legible against whatever page background is behind it.
- **"Poppy" in the headline is italic AND background-highlighted** (a light chip behind the word), rather than just a color change — guarantees contrast across all four hero background colors (orange, brown, pink, gold) where a plain color swap could wash out on at least one of them.

## Real photography — Clock Out & Gold Standard

The client supplied real packaging photography for two of the four hero products. `ImageBox` now accepts an optional `src`; when a product's `realImage` field is set, the component renders the real photo (via `next/image`, `object-contain`) instead of the grey placeholder, everywhere that product appears — hero, shelf pages, bestseller/all-drinks cards, multipacks, its own product page — not just the hero, since it's the same shared component everywhere.

- **Gold Standard's format was corrected to match the real bottle.** The supplied photo is a 60ml shot labeled "Sea Buckthorn + Ginger + Lemon — Energy · Immunity · Vitality," not a 250/500ml juice as I'd guessed when inventing it for the hero brief. Updated to `category: "shot"`, `60ml` single + `box of 10`, same pricing tier as the other boosters. It now also appears in the Reset shelf's shots row and the Multipacks page's booster section, which is more accurate than where it sat before.
- **Clock Out's photo confirmed the existing 250/500ml shake assumption** — no structural change, just wired in the real image.
- **The remaining 28 products still use placeholders** until Phase 6 (or further supplied photography) — nothing else was regenerated or guessed at.

## Direction v3 — hero

The client sent a reference animation (a Figma prototype export, ice-cream brand "FROSTÉ") and asked for the homepage hero to follow it closely, swapped to Poppy's own products and swiping left/right instead of up/down.

- **Four new flagship products added** — one per state, each described by the client with real ingredients and a true product color: Bright Idea (Wake Up, orange/ginger/turmeric), Clock Out (Refuel, coffee protein shake), Pink Noise (Cool Down, raspberry kombucha), Gold Standard (Reset, sea buckthorn/ginger/lemon, seasonal). Added as real catalog entries on their state shelves, not hero-only props, so "Shop Now" leads to a real product page. This brings the catalog to 30 items — the top of the stated 12–30 range; noted rather than trimmed, since the client asked to add, not replace.
- **Hero background colors are true-to-product, not the site's branded pastels.** The client described each product's actual color (bright orange, caramel-brown, bright pink, golden-orange) and asked the background to match it. These four hex values live only in the hero (`--hero-*` tokens) and are deliberately different from the sitewide `--wake/--refuel/--cool/--reset` pastels established in Direction v2 — the hero is marketing imagery meant to look like the real bottles; the rest of the site is calmer UI chrome. Both systems share the four states conceptually, just not one literal palette.
- **New serif face (Playfair Display) added for the wordmark only.** The reference's logotype is an elegant high-contrast serif, clearly a deliberate brand choice, not a generic sans. Applied to the shared `PoppyMark` component so the same "Poppy" logotype appears everywhere (nav, hero) rather than two different logo treatments existing on the site. Headlines and product names elsewhere keep Bricolage Grotesque — only the literal brand mark changed face.
- **Giant center wordmark is a fixed layer, not duplicated per slide.** In the reference, the brand name's position doesn't change as the flavor changes — only the product, background color and caption swap. Implemented as one constant centered "Poppy," with the product photo sliding in front of it (and partially covering it), matching the reference's own layering.
- **Nav redesigned as a floating white pill** (logo left, centered link pill, icon circles right), matching the reference's chrome. It overlays transparently on the homepage hero (logo in white) and sits in normal flow with an ink logo on every other page — same component, one `usePathname` check, so it's genuinely one nav design rather than two.
- **Search icon dropped from the nav**, matching the reference exactly (account + cart only). Nothing in scope currently needs on-site search.
- **"Our Story" renamed to "About Us"** in both the nav and footer, matching the client's wording, for consistency rather than having two different labels for the same page.
- **Mobile gets a simplified stacked layout, not the same overlapping composition scaled down.** The reference is a wide desktop frame; translating the exact overlap to a narrow phone width made the headline collide with the product image. Mobile shows image, then copy, in normal flow, with the giant wordmark hidden — the carousel mechanics (swipe, arrows, dots) are unchanged.
- **Announcement bar moves again** (continuous marquee), reversing the "static" decision from the previous round — the client explicitly asked for it back.

## Direction v2

The client redirected after check-in 2: away from the minimal "four states as the only nav, no format anywhere" structure, toward a fuller, more conventional-but-bold Shopify homepage with a complete section set (hero, four full-width state sections, bestsellers, multipacks, seasonal, delivery, origin story, gear, wholesale, newsletter, footer) and "Shop by State" as the lead discovery path alongside standard collections (All Drinks, Multipacks, Bottles & Gear). This brief is detailed and directive enough that it **is** the direction and much of the art direction — logged here rather than re-run through Phases 2–4 from scratch.

- **No star ratings on product cards.** The new brief suggests an "optional verified customer rating," but the fixed project scope explicitly excludes user reviews. Scope rules outrank a single creative suggestion inside an otherwise visual brief; omitted.
- **State colors are not 1:1 with the old palette.** New direction explicitly asks each state section to have "its own distinct, fun, saturated background color" without forcing a rigid single-brand-color-per-state system. Chosen: Wake Up — electric orange `#FF5A1F`; Refuel — vivid cobalt `#1E3AF2`; Cool Down — vivid turquoise `#00D9C0`; Reset — hot magenta `#FF1F7A`. Ink `#15120F` / paper `#FFFBF5` carry the neutral pages; the same near-black doubles as the deliberate dark background for Multipacks and Newsletter (contrast sections, not a state color).
- **Typography kept from the prior pass:** Bricolage Grotesque (display) + Schibsted Grotesk (body) — both already fit "oversized, confident, a little unexpected," no reason to change under the new direction.
- **Motion kept CSS-only (no GSAP/Lenis dependency added).** The new brief asks for marquees, hover states and "energetic transitions" but doesn't require a specific library, and the project doesn't have one installed. Implemented as a CSS marquee for the announcement bar, hover scale/opacity on cards and buttons, and `prefers-reduced-motion` fallbacks — meets the brief without adding a dependency for a single homepage pass.
- **Supporting pages built as real (if simple) destinations, not dead links**, per the brief's own "no dead-end mockups" rule: `/all-drinks`, `/multipacks`, `/wholesale`, `/faq`, `/contact` added alongside the existing `/wake-up` etc., `/gear`, `/about`, `/delivery`, `/cart`.
- **Navbar and Footer moved into the root layout** so every page shares one announcement bar / nav / footer instead of each page re-declaring them — pure implementation cleanup, not a design decision.
- **Quick-add on product cards is a visual stub**, consistent with the existing scope decision that cart/checkout state is designed, not wired, in this pass.
- **Bestsellers are all core (non-seasonal) products** — Rooftop Alarm, Loud Hour, Heavy Lifter, Full Tank, Cold Snap, Hard Reset — one from most states/formats, nothing that could sell out mid-season on a page framed as "what people come back for."

## Direction v1 (superseded 14 Oct 2026)

The original direction — kept here so the earlier reasoning isn't lost, not because it's still in effect.

- **Body typeface swapped from a generic pairing to Schibsted Grotesk.** It's a free, Google-Fonts-hosted grotesque designed for a Norwegian media company — gives the body face a real, citable reason for a Bergen brand instead of "it's clean and modern." Paired with Bricolage Grotesque for display. *(Kept under v2.)*
- **Site shows the current rotation (16 drinks), not the full annual rotation (~24).** The brief describes a year's worth of flavor-calendar items (sea buckthorn in autumn, rhubarb in June, black carrot in January). Showing all of them at once as "available" would be a factual inconsistency and would also blow past a believable shelf size. Core 8 + this season's 8 rotating = 16 drinks live on the site; rhubarb and black carrot are referenced in copy as returning items, not sold as current stock. *(Kept under v2.)*
- **Exactly two products are cross-listed across states** (Heavy Lifter: Refuel + Reset; Rinse: Cool Down + Reset), not every product — makes the point without erasing the sort. *(Kept under v2 — the new brief explicitly allows products in multiple state collections.)*
- **Six-pack option is limited to three hero juices** (Rooftop Alarm, Heavy Lifter, Hard Reset), matching the brief's own wording ("a few of the pressed juices also come as a six-pack").
- **A standalone bundle, "The Whole Board,"** was added as one extra product — a mixed six pulling from all four states, named after the literal four-state production board described in the brief. *(Kept under v2 as "The Mixed Six" framing in the Multipacks section.)*
- **Trade/wholesale (cafés + two gyms) gets one line and nothing else** — no portal, no login, consistent with the brief wanting it to "stay quiet" and with the fixed scope excluding wholesale portals.
- **Hardware/gear products use a distinct naming convention ("The Refill," "The Jug," "The Tamp"...)** separate from the playful one-word drink names, so tools read as plain-spoken workshop objects rather than competing with the drinks for personality.
- **Reset was the one state that inverted to a dark field (ink, not a bright hue)** under v1, earned by the brief's own charcoal-lemon-cayenne booster. *(Superseded — v2 gives Reset its own bright hot-magenta field instead; the dark field is now used for Multipacks/Newsletter, not tied to a single state.)*
