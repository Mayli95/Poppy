# Poppy — Client Brief

## Who we are

Poppy makes cold-pressed juice, smoothies, kombucha, protein shakes and wellness boosters in Bergen. We started out of a cargo bike at Torgallmenningen three winters ago, pressing in a rented kitchen in Møhlenpris overnight and selling until we ran out, which was usually by eleven. Now we press out of our own space in Solheimsviken and sell from two fridges in the city centre — plus, increasingly, to people who order online and want it at their door the same week.

The thing we are stubborn about: we do not sell drinks by format. We sell them by what they do for you. A customer standing in front of our fridge does not think "I would like a kombucha." They think "I need to wake up" or "I am wrecked and I need to reset." So everything we make lives in one of four states: **Wake Up**, **Refuel**, **Cool Down**, **Reset**. A kombucha and a smoothie can absolutely live in the same state, and the same ingredient shows up in two states doing two different jobs. This is not a marketing frame we bolted on afterwards. It is literally how the production board in Solheimsviken is laid out, and it is how we want the site to work. If someone has to click into "Kombucha" to find out whether it will help them, we've lost.

## The line

Roughly two dozen drinks, rotating. We keep a core of about eight that never leave, and the rest rotate with what's good — sea buckthorn from Hardanger in autumn, rhubarb in June, black carrot in January because nothing else is happening. Flavours are bold and we don't apologise for them: beetroot-ginger-sour cherry, celery-pear-dill, charcoal-lemon-cayenne. We lose a few customers to that. We keep more.

Most drinks come in two sizes, 250ml and 500ml, and a few of the pressed juices also come as a six-pack in one flavour or mixed. Boosters are small 60ml shots — turmeric-black pepper, ginger-cayenne, chlorophyll-mint — sold singly or in tens. Prices run roughly 45–89 kr for a single drink, 240–520 kr for multipacks.

Alongside the drinks we sell a small amount of hardware: three sizes of reusable glass bottle with a silicone sleeve (four sleeve colours), a stainless pressing jug, a tamper, a bottle brush, and a cloth bag. These exist because people kept asking where to get our bottles. They're not the business but they are good margin and they look great in a photo.

Delivery is the part we worry about. Everything is chilled and unpasteurised, so it goes out in insulated boxes on fixed days — Bergen city and Åsane/Fyllingsdalen get next-day, the rest of Vestland is a two-day window, and anything further north we won't send at all. The cut-off is 14:00 the day before. Customers get this wrong constantly: they order a 500ml beetroot on a Friday afternoon and expect it Saturday. Juice has a seven-day fridge life from pressing, and the day it was pressed is stamped on the cap, not a best-before guess. We want people to understand that before they pay, not in an email afterwards.

A small side of this is cafés and two gyms that take crates weekly. It's under a tenth of what we do and we'd rather it stayed quiet — a line on the site is enough.

## What the site has to do

Get someone who has never heard of us from "I feel like rubbish" to a box at their door without them having to learn our product range first. Make the four states the way in, not a tag. Make the delivery rules impossible to misunderstand. And sell the six-packs harder — single bottles are a thin order and the boxes are the same cost to ship.

## Taste

We love **100coconuts.com** — the colour confidence, the way the product is enormous on screen and nothing is whispering. That's the energy. Lean on it. **bonbuz.com** is funny without being childish. **mahati.co** does something with flat colour and shape that feels designed by a person, not assembled. **drinkolipop.com** and **drinkculturepop.com** both get boldness right without looking like a children's party. **delacalle.mx** has real personality. The Shopify "Taste" theme is the baseline of competence we want to beat.

What we don't want: **actiphwater.com** is the exact wrong thing — grey, cold, boring, clinical, could be selling anything. **drinkmoment.com**, **sunwink.com**, **aurabora.com** and **drinkdaydream.com** all have the same problem to us: soft, pastel, calm, apologetic. We are not a calm brand. We are a bright, loud, clean brand with no clutter. Fun and colourful but modern and bold — not a cartoon, not scrapbook, not twee.

## What exists

A wordmark (lowercase "poppy", one weight), a four-colour palette we've used on cup sleeves and labels which honestly may be too close to the four states to keep separate, label artwork for the eight core drinks, an A1 poster from the first market stall, a photo archive of about 600 shots from the Solheimsviken space and the market days, and the production board layout itself. We have never had a real website — just an Instagram and a Google Maps pin.
