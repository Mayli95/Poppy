> **Direction v2, in one paragraph:** Poppy's homepage leads with brand impact (hero), then the four states as the primary discovery path — but now as four immersive, independently colored full-width sections rather than a minimal nav-only system — followed by bestsellers, a dedicated multipacks section (the main conversion lever), seasonal rotation, an unmissable delivery/freshness block, the origin story, gear, a quiet wholesale mention, and a newsletter signup. Standard collection entry points (All Drinks, Multipacks, Bottles & Gear) sit alongside Shop by State in the nav — format isn't hidden the way v1 insisted, but state is still positioned first and given by far the most homepage real estate. The four states each get their own fully saturated, distinct color rather than sharing one restrained palette, because the brief wants every section to feel like "a bold chapter," not a quiet wayfinding system.

## v1 (superseded) — The choice

The obvious version of this site is the one every competitor already runs: a hero banner, then "Shop" opening a dropdown of Juice / Kombucha / Smoothies / Shots / Bottles, then a grid you filter by format and size. Pressed Juicery and Suja both already prove that a benefit-based collection (energy, calm, gut health) can coexist with that format grid — but in both cases it's a secondary filter bolted on top, never the way in. Poppy's own production board already sorts everything by Wake Up / Refuel / Cool Down / Reset, not by format — that division is real, it predates the website, and a kombucha and a smoothie are deliberately allowed to sit in the same state doing different jobs. So the structural idea is: **the four states are the only front door.** There is no "Shop" menu that opens onto formats. You land on a state, not a product type, and format only shows up once you're looking at a specific drink. The thing I am deliberately not building is a format-first catalog with "shop by mood" as a filter chip somewhere in the sidebar — that pattern exists already (Pressed, Suja) and it still asks the customer to think in formats first.

## The bet

The bet: customers already think "I need to wake up," not "I'd like a kombucha," so removing the format layer entirely — making it invisible until the product page — will move more six-packs than a cleaner filter would, because it never makes the customer translate their own problem into our taxonomy.

## How it works

**Navigation.** Wordmark left. Primary nav is the four state names only — Wake Up, Refuel, Cool Down, Reset — each one tinted to its own color on hover/active. "Shop All" and "Gear" (the bottles/hardware) sit smaller, to the right, visibly secondary. On mobile the four states stay visible as a horizontal color-pill strip under the header on every page — they never collapse into a hamburger, because they are the whole point.

**Catalog entry.** The homepage is four enormous, full-bleed, full-color sections, one per state, each a "room" in that state's color with its biggest product filling the frame — no shared white backdrop, no small thumbnails. Each section leads to that state's own shelf page, which is a single long product run (juice next to kombucha next to a shot, mixed on purpose) rather than a filtered grid. A drink that belongs to two states is shown in full in both, with a small repeating tag pointing to the other state it lives in — the overlap is a feature, shown, not hidden.

**Product page.** The product sits on a field of its own state's color (the color is the backdrop system everywhere, not just on the homepage). Size/pack picker, an intensity cue for anything fermented (the research shows buyers think in "light / tangy / big pucker," not flavor names, before they commit), and — set apart in plain ink-on-paper, deliberately uncolored so it reads as fact, not marketing — the pressed-on date, the delivery day it will actually arrive, and the cutoff. Six-packs get the dominant position on every state shelf; single bottles are smaller and secondary.

**Density.** State landing pages are sparse and oversized — one product commanding the screen at a time. Product pages are denser: image, picker, intensity tag, delivery fact block, ingredients, cross-state link.

**Type:** Bricolage Grotesque (display — a grotesque with enough personality in its curves to carry huge, confident state headlines without reading as another geometric sans-serif default) + General Sans (body — clean, humanist, unremarkable on purpose so it never competes with the display face or the color).

**Palette:** Off-white paper #FAF7F2 and ink #161616 as the neutral structure everywhere. Three states run hot and bright — Wake Up #FF4428 (blood-orange/citrus), Refuel #FFB400 (golden amber), Cool Down #0FBFAE (cold teal). Reset inverts to the ink #161616 as its field color with a single acid-lemon #E8FF5C accent — earned by the brief's own charcoal-lemon-cayenne booster, and the one deliberate dark note in an otherwise loud site. Each color stays inside its own state's territory; nothing borrows another state's color.

**Homepage copy, three lines:**
"Four moods. Four fridges, basically. Pick the one you're in."
"We don't care if it's a kombucha or a smoothie — we care if it gets you where you're going."
"Bergen box, cold the whole way, pressed the day it says on the cap."

## The lift-test

Could this be reskinned onto an unrelated business by swapping logo and palette? No — the structure depends on a specific content fact (one product, multiple states, shown in full in each) and a specific trust fact (pressed-on date + delivery day as a first-class, uncolored block on every product). Neither transplants onto a business without that same shape of problem.
